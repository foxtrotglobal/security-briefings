NATIONWIDE THANKSGIVING BRIEFING, 23 November 2026. This briefing covers the whole country for Thanksgiving week: the national threat picture, Iran, lone actors, drones, major events, travel, weather, cyber and scams, protests, and a region-by-region rundown.

Date: Monday 23 November 2026 | Classification: UNCLASSIFIED // OSINT
Coverage: United States, Tue 24 Nov to Mon 30 Nov 2026 (Thanksgiving is Thu 26 Nov). All times are ET with CT in parentheses.
Research window: 30 Oct to 23 Nov 2026 (sources accessed 23 Nov 2026).

How to read this report. [FACT] means a cited source confirms the item. [SINGLE-SOURCE] means only one outlet reports it. [OPINION] means commentary. [ASSESSMENT] means the author's analytic judgment. Likelihood terms follow ICD 203: remote (1–5%), very unlikely (5–20%), unlikely (20–45%), roughly even (45–55%), likely (55–80%), very likely (80–95%). Source numbers in [brackets] are clickable and jump to the Sources section. This report deliberately leaves out operational attack detail.

1. Bottom Line

National rating: ELEVATED. That is unchanged. For this week, the most certain disruptions are weather and travel, not a known threat. (Moderate confidence.)

DHS has no current NTAS advisory, and no public FBI or DHS warning names a specific, credible threat to a Thanksgiving event [1]. The bigger U.S.–Iran escalation expected after the midterms has not happened. Fighting in the Gulf is tit-for-tat, and President Trump says he is inclined to resume strikes after the new year if talks fail [2][3]. Federal officials told Congress that Iranian lethal plotting has "dramatically increased," but the reported dirty-bomb planning against Washington is unconfirmed and described as largely "aspirational" [4][5]. The most realistic violent threat to crowds stays the self-radicalized lone actor (vehicle, firearm, or knife) at soft perimeters [6][7]. A two-coast storm pattern is the week's biggest certainty: a nor'easter brings heavy rain and 40–50 mph gusts from the Southeast to the Northeast on Wed–Thu, and rain and mountain snow hit the West through the weekend. Both are likely to cause flight delays during a near-record travel week [8][9][10].

2. National Threat Level and DHS/FBI Posture [FACT]

  • NTAS: "There are no current advisories." The last Iran-related bulletin expired on 22 Sep 2025 [1]. We found no public 2026 joint FBI/DHS holiday-season bulletin.
  • Worldwide Threats hearing (week of 16 Nov):
    • DNI Clayton said Iranian lethal plotting inside and outside the U.S. has "dramatically increased."
    • FBI Deputy Director Raia said much of the reporting is "largely aspirational" but taken seriously, and that the FBI has revamped its Iranian threat mission center.
    • DHS Secretary Mullin called possible sleeper cells "a concern" [4][5].
    • Raia also said the FBI created a fusion cell for nihilistic violent extremism (NVE), the youth-driven online networks tied to mass-attack plotting [5].
  • Iranian-backed operatives indicted: The FBI said a joint New York–Washington investigation produced an indictment of three Iranian-backed operatives for nearly 20 attacks and attempted attacks across Europe and the United States [5]. Separately, the Kata'ib Hizballah defendant in the Manhattan synagogue-plot case pleaded not guilty to expanded charges on 19 Nov [11].
  • Tech outages: DHS, ODNI and the FBI told Congress that this month's outages (airlines, banks, Microsoft, an NSA network, and the FAA's 13 Nov ground stop) were separate maintenance problems, not a coordinated or state-sponsored cyberattack [4][12].

4. Domestic Extremism, Lone Actors and Vehicle Ramming

  • [FACT] Recent U.S. cases:
    • Aurora, Illinois: a 19-year-old with an alleged white-supremacist manifesto was indicted (9 Nov) on terrorism-threat and weapons charges. He allegedly told a coworker he had gone to attack a large Hindu temple on 11 Oct but his gun jammed; police say video shows his car in the temple lot [7].
    • Mall of America: the ISIS-support plot suspect (arrested 6 Oct) remains charged [17].
    • Overseas: Krakow, Poland (reported 7–8 Nov): a gunman officials believe was likely influenced by far-right ideology killed six in the main square [18].
  • [FACT] Vehicle-ramming precedent: The 1 Jan 2025 New Orleans Bourbon Street truck attack (ISIS-inspired, acting alone per the FBI) killed 14 [6]. On 17 Oct 2026, a truck drove through a crowd at a youth soccer game in Wichita, killing two; police had not stated a motive in the reporting reviewed [19].
  • [ASSESSMENT] Parades, Black Friday crowds and stadium tailgates are classic soft targets. Major parade routes (NYC, Philadelphia, Detroit, Chicago, Houston) use closures and barriers. The residual risk sits at unscreened side streets, queues, parking lots, and dispersal. A lone-actor attack causing casualties at a U.S. holiday-week event is very unlikely (5–10%). Hoaxes, swatting, and false-alarm stampedes are more likely than real attacks.

5. Drones [FACT unless noted]

  • On 9 Nov, DHS, DOT, the Department of War, DOE and DOJ signed an expanded counter-drone agreement. The Attorney General cited an FBI arrest this summer of five men accused of planning to use explosive-armed drones around a White House event [20].
  • The FBI said it seized more than 700 drones during the 2026 World Cup [20]. The DHS Secretary later said more than 2,000 were "encountered," and admitted to "holes" in the ability to bring down drones (SINGLE-SOURCE, trade press) [21].
  • The FBI says its National Counter-UAS Training Center is deputizing state and local agencies to detect and mitigate drones [5].
  • Drones are banned at NYC's Balloon Inflation event [22], and the NYPD used its own drones at the 2025 parade [23]. We found no public FAA flight restriction (TFR) list specific to the week's parades; stadium games normally fall under standing NFL/NCAA restrictions (ASSESSMENT; check B4UFLY/FAA before flying).
  • [ASSESSMENT] The most likely drone problem this week is an unauthorized hobby drone that pauses a game or parade segment, not a weaponized drone.

[ASSESSMENT] Stadium screening (clear-bag policies, magnetometers) makes inside-the-gate attacks unlikely. The realistic risks are crowd crush at gates, weather exposure, alcohol-fueled violence in tailgate lots, and vehicle or pedestrian conflicts at dispersal. For retail, expect isolated fights or brawls over door-buster items, parking-lot robberies, and hoax "shooter" alarms that cause stampedes. These are recurring holiday-season patterns; we found no 2026-specific warning.

  • Workers and crews at live events: Anyone working, reporting, or broadcasting at a parade, stadium, or mall should have a set check-in, a fallback meeting spot, and a plan for gear in heavy rain and wind (ASSESSMENT).
  • Information hygiene: Rumors ("sleeper cells," "cyberattack" outages) spread fast during holiday news cycles. Officials say recent outages were not attacks. Verify before sharing [4][39].
  • Cyber: Fake-outage "reset your password" phishing and spoofed airline or bank calls are expected to exploit the recent outages. Work, email and social accounts need MFA [39].

8. Air and Road Travel [FACT unless noted]

  • Volume:
    • AAA projects 81.5 million people traveling 50+ miles from 24 to 30 Nov (slightly below 2025's record), including 72.9M by car and 5.96M by air. INRIX says New York, D.C., Los Angeles and Boston could see more than double normal congestion [40].
    • TSA says it could screen more than 3 million people nationwide on Sun 29 Nov [10]. The all-time record is 3,133,924 on Sun 30 Nov 2025 [41]. TSA's daily table for 16–22 Nov shows about 1.96 to 2.78 million screened per day [42].
  • Hubs:
    • Atlanta (ATL): TSA expects peak days Tue–Wed and Sun, with more than 95,000 screened on Sunday. Arrive 2–3 hours early [10].
    • NYC-area airports: about 3.1M passengers expected 23–30 Nov, with Wed 25 and Mon 30 Nov busiest. Non-REAL-ID licenses need a passport or the $45 ConfirmID process [43].
    • Newark and LaGuardia already saw weather delays and a ground stop on 22 Nov [44].
  • FAA/ATC:
    • The 13 Nov nationwide ground stop (about 3:30–8:15 PM ET, 2:30–7:15 PM CT) came from network flooding linked to a contractor's router work. The FAA says there is no indication of a cyberattack; about 12,462 flights were canceled that day [12].
    • Controller shortages remain a chronic weak point: per Cronkite News, nearly 75% of FAA facilities are below desired staffing [45].
  • Driving: Worst windows are Tue 2–8 PM, Wed noon–7 PM, and Sat/Sun noon–8/9 PM (local time); best before noon [40].
  • [ASSESSMENT] Thursday flights across the Mid-Atlantic and Northeast, and Sunday flights in California, carry the highest weather-delay risk. Another FAA technical stop cannot be ruled out but is unlikely.

10. Cyber and Holiday Scams [FACT unless noted]

  • The FBI (Boston office, 19 Nov) warns of online-shopping, gift-card and charity scams. Red flags: spelling errors, deals that are too good to be true, and urgency. AI is making fake storefronts easier to build [46].
  • Expect fake shipping notices, cloned retailer sites, gift-card tampering, and outage-themed "reset your password" or spoofed bank and airline contacts. Report fraud to ic3.gov or reportfraud.ftc.gov [39].
  • [ASSESSMENT] If U.S. strikes on Iran resume, pro-Iran hacktivist DDoS attacks and defacements against U.S. media and retail sites become likely. That is not expected this week given the reported strike delay.

13. Recommendations (non-operational)

  • Travel timing: Fly or drive before noon Wednesday where possible. Avoid Thursday afternoon and evening in the Mid-Atlantic and Northeast, and Sunday in California. Arrive 2–3 hours early at major hubs. Carry a REAL ID or passport. Check FAA status (nasstatus.faa.gov) and your airline app before leaving [40][10][43][12].
  • Events: Use a buddy system and set check-in times. Pick a primary and a backup rally point away from parade routes, stadium gates, and dispersal flows, indoors if it's raining. Know the venue bag rules (clear bags at NFL stadiums; small bags only in Detroit's grandstands; no umbrellas in NYC parade viewing areas or Detroit's grandstands) [34][27][22].
  • Communications: Keep a backup channel (SMS plus a messaging app) for cell congestion. Sign up for local alert systems (Notify NYC, ReadyPhiladelphia, etc.). Follow local police and NWS accounts.
  • What to avoid: Standing near balloon paths, scaffolding, or loose signage in high wind. Flood-prone and coastal streets at high tide. Crowded parking lots after games. Door-buster stampedes. Flying any drone near events.
  • Information discipline: Don't share unconfirmed "attack," "sleeper cell," or "cyberattack" claims. Confirm with DHS, FBI, or local police first [4].
  • Cyber: Use MFA on email, work and social accounts. Expect outage-themed phishing and spoofed calls. Verify payment and vendor changes by phone [39][46].
  • Report suspicious activity to on-site police or 911.
  • Look ahead: Re-check venue rules and weather before early-December events, and re-assess the Iran picture on 1 Dec.
